# Deep QA — the product from a tenant's chair

**2026-09-04.** Companion to `2026-09-04-plan.md`, which says *how* we test.
This one says *what a shop actually does*, and it is built around the four
things you asked for:

1. every business type manages everything properly
2. **no tenant, and no branch, ever sees anybody else's anything**
3. the plans the admin sets are the plans the tenant feels
4. each and everything

---

## Part 1 — Eight shops, eight full days

Not a feature checklist. Each of these is **one shop, opened at 9am and closed
at night**, driven end to end — because the bugs that reach a shop are almost
never in a feature, they are in the seam between two of them.

Every journey ends with the same four questions, which is where the money
actually goes wrong:

> **Does the drawer balance? Does the shelf match? Does the report say the same
> number as the screen? Is the audit trail able to explain it tomorrow?**

The modules each trade is GIVEN by default (verified against
`BusinessTypes::defaultFeatures`, not assumed):

| Trade | Modules on | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **food** | products · pos · customers · expenses · images · marketplace · delivery · kitchen · dine_in | 9 |
| **mart** | + inventory · purchasing · stocktake · disposals · labels · promotions | 13 |
| **pharmacy** | products · pos · inventory · purchasing · stocktake · disposals · customers · expenses · delivery | 9 |
| **retail** | the widest — + documents · reservations | 15 |
| **services** | services · pos · documents · customers · expenses | 5 |
| **automotive** | products · services · pos · documents · inventory · purchasing · stocktake · customers · expenses | 9 |
| **petroleum** | + fuel | 9 |
| **finance** | expenses **only** | 1 |

### 1.1 Restaurant (food) — a full service
Open shift → walk-in rings a takeaway → **KOT prints** → a table is seated →
items fired in two rounds → a dish 86'd mid-service (the till, the floor AND
the online menu must all refuse it) → bill split three ways, one paying by
card → tab settled → an online delivery order arrives and is accepted → close.
**Watch:** the takeaway KOT and the dine-in KOT are the same kitchen; a
cancelled tab must not leave a docket on the pass; `kot_waiting` must return
to zero.

### 1.2 Mart — a delivery day
Receive a purchase order (part-delivered) → cost re-blends → labels printed for
the new pack → a stock count finds two short → a promotion runs → a customer
buys on khata → a damaged case is disposed of → close.
**Watch:** cost blending is weighted-average per **base unit**; a stock count
and a disposal must not both claim the same two units.

### 1.3 Pharmacy — a prescription and a recall
Sell a scheduled medicine (Rx captured) → FEFO picks the nearest expiry → the
same drug is ordered **online** (the second door — it must enforce the same
schedule rule) → a batch is recalled → near-expiry alerts speak **once**, not
daily.
**Watch:** a medicine batch with no expiry must be refused; a substitution by
salt must not cross a schedule.

### 1.4 Retail — the widest shop
A quotation → converted to an invoice → a layaway with a deposit → the deposit
is drawer cash but **never revenue** → a serialised phone sold with IMEI →
returned under warranty → a reservation held and collected.
**Watch:** a deposit settles as its own tender at conversion; layaway stock
leaves at deposit time and must not leave twice.

### 1.5 Services — five modules and nothing else
A job quoted → done → invoiced → paid. **And then: every screen the other
seven trades have must be absent** — no inventory, no purchasing, no labels.
**Watch:** this is the trade most likely to be offered a job it cannot do.

### 1.6 Automotive — a vehicle, not a customer
A car arrives → job card → parts **and** labour on one ticket → an old battery
taken in part-exchange → the vehicle's history shows it next time.
**Watch:** a trade-in is a **TENDER, not a discount** — the bill must not shrink.

### 1.7 Petroleum — a shift, not a day
Open the forecourt → rate changes at midnight → fuel sold **by rupees**
("do hazaar ka") → test litres pumped and tipped back → close on a **dip in
millimetres** → the two variances (meter-vs-till, book-vs-dip) are reported
**separately and never summed** → the month's fuel report agrees with the shifts.
**Watch:** all three of today's new paths; and a mart attached to the pump.

### 1.8 Finance — one module
Expenses only. The whole point is what is **absent**: no till, no shelf, no
reports it can never fill. Seven of ten report tabs must not be offered.

## Part 2 — Isolation, proven rather than assumed

This is the part you asked about most directly, and it is the part with the
most surface. There are **three separate fences** and they fail differently.

### 2.1 The tenant fence

`BelongsToTenant` puts a global scope on every query and auto-fills
`tenant_id`, and creating without a tenant context is a hard error. That is the
strong half. The weak halves are these, and each gets tested by hand:

**(a) 15 models sit OUTSIDE the fence.** Some correctly — a Plan, a City, a
Banner and the Tenant itself belong to the platform. Others carry a tenant and
are fenced **by hand**, which means the fence can be forgotten on the next
endpoint somebody adds:

```
Announcement  AppNotification  AuditLog  Banner  City  CustomerAddress
DeviceToken   Enquiry  OtpCode  Plan  ShopRequest  SubscriptionPayment
Tenant        User     BaseModel
```

Already read: `AppNotification` is fenced by `user_id`; `AuditLog` is fenced by
an explicit `where('tenant_id')` in the tenant controller with a comment saying
why. **Every one of the fifteen gets asked the same question by test:** sign in
as shop A, ask for shop B's row by id, and require a 404 — not an empty list,
which can also mean "the query was wrong".

**(b) 187 hand-written `where('tenant_id')` fences.** Every one is a place the
fence was applied on purpose, which means every one is a place it could have
been left out. The endpoint sweep asks all of them from the wrong shop.

**(c) 130 `withoutTenancy()` calls — the deliberate holes.** Four are on the
**tenant** side, and I have read all four: each is immediately re-fenced with an
explicit `where('tenant_id', …)`. That is the rule, and because it is a rule it
becomes a **scanner**: *a `withoutTenancy()` on the tenant side must be followed
by an explicit tenant_id, or it is a finding.*

### 2.2 The branch fence

Nine models carry `branch_id`. A branch is a **sub-unit, not a tenant** — one
catalogue, separate stock, separate prices, separate drawers. So the question is
not "can branch two read branch one" (sometimes it must — cross-branch stock
lookup is a feature) but **"can branch two SPEND, SELL or COUNT branch one's
stock"**.

Tested as a real pair of shops under one owner:
- a cashier pinned to branch two sells → branch two's stock falls, branch one's does not
- a hand stock adjustment lands on the **operating** branch, not always Main
- branch prices override tenant prices for **retail only**, never wholesale
- a transfer leaves one and arrives at the other, and is not double-counted
- the day, the drawer and the shift are per branch; **HQ rolls up, and the roll-up equals the sum**
- a table, a tank, a pump and a nozzle each belong to a site
- an owner in the "all branches" view **writes** to a concrete branch, never to none

### 2.3 The permission fence

Every screen asked as six different people: owner · manager · cashier · waiter ·
kitchen · finance. Two failure directions, and **both** are bugs:

- a person who should be refused is let in
- a person who should be let in is refused — **the `*.manage` bug class**: a
  READ gated behind a WRITE permission. This has already happened here more than
  once, and it looks like "the feature is broken" rather than like a permission.

## Part 3 — The plan the admin sets is the plan the tenant feels

Limits that exist: `products · staff · branches · registers · orders_month ·
storage_mb · offline_selling · offline_days · offline_hard_stop_days`.

For each, the same three-step test, because a limit has three places to be
wrong and only the first is usually checked:

1. **Admin sets it** → the tenant's own screen shows the new figure
2. **The tenant hits it** → the refusal names the limit and says what to do,
   rather than failing generically
3. **The admin raises it** → the tenant can proceed **without signing out**
   (a stale session that still believes the old limit is the classic bug here)

And the reverse, which is the one nobody tests: **the admin LOWERS a limit below
what the shop already has.** Four branches on a plan that now allows two. The
answer must be a stated rule — existing rows keep working, new ones are refused
— and not a crash or a silent deletion.

Also: a module switched **off** by the admin must vanish from every surface at
once — the menu, the dashboard tiles, the report tabs, the settings tabs, the
routes and the API. That is four places, not one, and it has leaked eight times.

## Part 4 — What "each and everything" means here

Beyond the journeys above, these are asked deliberately because they are where
this product has actually broken before:

- **absence** — an optional field left out where the column has a default
  (three live defects from this one class)
- **a rule written in a comment and implemented nowhere** (four found in one day)
- **the same rule answered in two places**, drifted (low stock: asked in 5
  places, answered 2 ways)
- **page two** — nine screens once could not reach it at all
- **an empty state** that is empty because the query was wrong, not because the
  shop has nothing
- **a save that says "Saved" and saved nothing** (25 of these were found at once)
- **time** — month-end, the 1st, midnight shifts, Karachi vs UTC
- **offline** — sell, reload, keep selling, come back; two tills at once; a rate
  that moved while the line was down

## Part 5 — What I will not claim

- **The mobile app** is excluded — it has no gates at all.
- **iOS Safari** cannot be run here; an old iPad is not reproducible on this Mac.
- **Load at scale** — the sweep runs two lanes, not two hundred.
- **Your live data.** Nothing destructive touches `panel.cartze.shop`. Any write
  on live is asked about first.
- **"Bug free"** is not something a run can prove. What it can prove is: this
  set of questions was asked, this many were answered wrong, and each of those
  is now fixed and has a test that fails if it comes back.
